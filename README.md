# The Tiny Town

Cosy isometric AI-driven village life simulation built with Unity 6 and local Ollama AI.

- Design: [GameDesignDocument.md](GameDesignDocument.md)
- Building, testing and downloading Windows builds: [Documentation/BuildAndTest.md](Documentation/BuildAndTest.md)

Status: Phase 5 (observer interface) build 2 of 3: camera panels, a fuller inspector, a searchable town journal, resident timelines, notifications and exported life stories. Phase 4 is complete. Ten residents live in an isometric village with ten cottages, a general store, a café, a bakery, a clinic and a repair workshop, all in placeholder art. Each has eight needs, a daily routine, and either a job with set days, hours and an hourly wage, or a pension or allowance. Money is tracked to the penny: wages are paid when a shift ends, rent and pensions settle every Monday morning, and every payment is recorded. Cooking at home uses groceries bought at the store; the café and bakery sell meals, coffee and pastries during opening hours while stock lasts, and anyone broke and out of food can still snack, so nobody starves. Residents meet, chat over several turns, form relationships and remember what happened. Each has two hobbies (painting, piano, chess, fishing, gardening, baking or knitting) and invites a neighbour to join the shared ones. Lifespans are compressed: a year lasts 28 game days, so everyone has birthdays and moves through life stages. Health is worn down by neglect and illness; colds and flu come and go (flu keeps people home in bed), flu in poor health can become pneumonia that needs the clinic, and very old age or extreme neglect can end a life. The dead stay in the town's records and are remembered by their neighbours. When a local Ollama service is running, the AI chooses their free-time activities (seeing prices and their money), writes their conversation lines and their end-of-day reflections; the game checks every answer and keeps all the rules, including money. Without Ollama they follow their built-in routine and use simple small talk. Adults who are open to it, close in age and have chemistry can fall for each other, date, commit, quarrel, break up and make up, but it is never guaranteed and can be switched off. The village calendar has a Sunday get-together, a Wednesday quiz night and a yearly fête; residents go when they fancy it, partners and good friends invite each other for coffee, and the panel keeps the village news. You watch through one to ten camera panels, each following a resident indoors and out or roaming freely, and the inspector on the right shows the chosen resident in tabs (now, people, memories, work, life), with anything the AI wrote marked as such. Controls: Space pauses, keys 1–4 set the speed, F5 saves, F9 loads. Click a panel to make it active; the name buttons make it follow someone, N and B step through residents, Tab turns it into a free camera and moves it to the next map, the "Place" button in a panel's header picks the village or any building for it to show, F follows the chosen resident, + and − add and remove panels, drag pans a free camera and the mouse wheel zooms. J opens the town journal: search it, filter it by topic or resident, and export the town history or a resident's life story as text. Big moments (deaths, couples, break-ups, new life stages, serious illness) pop up as notifications.
